# Watertown Chamber Candidate Forum — Moderator Question Scorecard
**Event:** Watertown Area Chamber of Commerce candidate forum  
**Date / place:** Oct 6, 2026 · Heritage Theater (Watertown Event Center)  
**Stream:** [Northeast Radio SD — District 4 & 5 Candidate Forum](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O36kPpEOOx4) (~1:34; also includes Codington County Auditor panel)  
**Prepared for:** Zach T / DOGE Civics · Oct 6, 2026 (CT)

**Rubric:** (a) Softball/feel-good · (b) Principle-testing · (c) Gotcha/unfair · (d) Substantive with follow-up  
**Source:** Local ASR of the livestream (YouTube had no captions). Names/terms may be slightly off; core asks are clear. Full question bank: `moderator-questions.md`. Transcript: `chamber-d45-transcript.txt`.

**Verdict up front:** This question set mostly fails voters. Soft and autobiographical prompts let candidates pick friendly ground; the few substantive asks (data centers, education tracks, constituent-input) almost never get a second question that forces a number, a tradeoff, or a clash. Compare that to District 2’s 2026 forum, which demanded yes/no and intra-party critique.

## Comparison to other SD forums (2024–2026)

### 1. [2026 District 2 Candidate Forum](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H2KXWr5vzus)
Much sharper. Examples: suspend gas tax yes/no; prioritize specific first-year bills; right to repair; **data centers with rural energy/water burden + “will you refuse a data-center job?”**; mental health / foster care; **“where is your own Republican Party getting it wrong?”**; school choice that strengthens vs weakens the district. That last intra-party question alone does more to reveal principle differences than Watertown’s entire D4/D5 block.

### 2. [Brookings Chamber District 7 House forum (Sep 30, 2024)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tICr1aVT3DI)
Chamber-hosted but still more ballot- and tradeoff-heavy: childcare shortage; **IM28 + how you balance the budget**; campus free speech / drag shows; Amendment E explain + yes/no; brain drain; committees. Forces positions on live ballot fights instead of open “name two issues.”

### 3. Also checked: [District 18 House & Senate (Oct 1, 2026)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m1phR1DGOYM) — property tax/housing, civility, teacher pay, “one thing you’d change.” Middling; still more concrete than Watertown’s committee + two-issues openers.

**Vs Watertown Chamber:** Same local Chamber format, but Watertown’s House panels lean hardest on résumé softballs (two issues, committees, proudest bill) and recycle one good process question (constituent input) without a second bite. Data centers appear — good — but without the District 2-style constraints that would separate cheerleaders from skeptics.

---

## Why this set fails voters

Soft questions let candidates hide behind rhetoric. “Name two issues,” “which committees,” “most important bill you’ve worked on,” and HR-style auditor prompts produce rehearsed bios, not binding commitments. The better prompts (hand-counting, budget “no,” ballot position, education tracks, data centers, constituent input) were left as one-shot answers with **almost no moderator follow-up that forced a number, a tradeoff, or a disagreement on the record**. Voters leave knowing tone and self-description, not who would cut what, tax what, site what, or override whom. For a Chamber night that also covers the county’s chief election/fiscal officer, that is a missed accountability window.

**What would have fixed it (illustrative, not asked tonight):** one follow-up each on data-center power/water/tax abatements; a yes/no on hand-count mandates vs machine audit; auditor budget “no” with a recent Codington example; education track with funding math; and one intra-party “where do allies get it wrong?” à la District 2.
