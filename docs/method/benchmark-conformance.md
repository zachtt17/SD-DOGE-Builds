# Benchmark Conformance — The Correspondence Scoring Instrument

**Status:** method v0.1, derived from Correspondence spec v0.3 plus the September 2026
repositioning. Not yet exercised on a full artifact.

**Scope:** scores officials, candidates, votes, ordinances, and ballot measures against a
declared founding-era benchmark set. **Not** for fiscal reviews — see §7.

---

## 1. What this instrument claims, and what it does not

**Claims:** that a specific public action can be compared to a specific, cited, dated argument
from the Western constitutional tradition, and that the degree of correspondence can be stated
on a published scale that a critic can re-run.

**Does not claim:** that the benchmark is neutral, that the founders would have agreed among
themselves, that conformance equals legality, or that a low score implies bad faith. The
instrument measures correspondence to a declared standard. It does not measure virtue.

**Authorship is disclosed on page one of every artifact, every time.** The rubric is
classical-liberal and Protestant in derivation. Presenting it as neutral civic education while
concealing the lineage is more damaging, when traced, than owning it would ever have been.

---

## 2. The benchmark frames

Each frame carries a **declared end-date** and the reason for it. A frame without a stated
terminus is an invitation to cherry-pick across two centuries, and it is the first thing a
serious critic will attack.

| # | Frame | Record closes | Why there |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** | Legal-structural | **1790s** | The ratification debate and the First Congress's own construction of the document it had just ratified — the founding generation legislating under its own text, through the Bill of Rights (1791) and the Alien and Sedition controversy (1798). After this, the ratifiers are no longer the actors. |
| **F2** | Jurisprudence | **Story** (*Commentaries*, 1833; d. 1845) | The last systematic constitutional commentary written by a jurist in living professional contact with the founding generation. After Story, interpretation is downstream of the sectional crisis and is arguing about something else. |
| **F3** | Civic | **1860s** | The union, citizenship, and equal-protection questions the founding record left genuinely unresolved are settled in this decade. A civic benchmark that stops in 1789 cannot speak to citizenship at all, and would be dishonest by omission. |
| **F4** | Education | **1880s** | Covers the common-school settlement and its contemporaneous critics, and closes *before* Progressive-era professionalization of schooling — which is the development the benchmark exists to evaluate, and therefore cannot be part of the standard. |
| **F5** | Economics | **Open-ended, through the Austrians** | The only frame that is a continuous live research program rather than a closed documentary record: Smith → Bastiat → Menger → Mises → Hayek. An arbitrary cutoff here would exclude the strongest form of the argument. The openness is itself declared, and is the frame most exposed to the cherry-picking charge — cite the *earliest* adequate statement of any principle used. |

> **Reconciliation item.** The frames and end-dates above are as recorded. The *rationales* are
> reconstructed from working context and must be reconciled against the wording in
> Correspondence spec v0.3 before any artifact publishes them. Where the spec's rationale
> differs, the spec wins.

### Corpus

Founding-era retrieval is built on **public corpora**: Founders Online, the Avalon Project,
the Library of Congress, and the Anti-Federalist corpus. These are load-bearing. Any
third-party or proprietary source, including George AI, is an **optional adapter behind an
interface** and no artifact may depend on one for a citation it cannot also source publicly.

Rule: **if a critic cannot reach the cited passage for free, the citation does not ship.**

## 3. The scale

Five bands plus a gap marker. Stated in full in every artifact so a reader can replicate a
placement.

| Band | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **+2** | Conforms | The action tracks a cited benchmark argument in both principle and effect |
| **+1** | Leans conforming | Consistent with the benchmark; effect or reasoning partly untested |
| **0** | Indeterminate | Benchmark record is genuinely divided, or the action is orthogonal to it |
| **−1** | Leans departing | In tension with a cited benchmark argument |
| **−2** | Departs | Contradicts a cited benchmark argument in principle and effect |
| **—** | **Insufficient public record** | No citable public action on this criterion |

**The gap marker is not a zero.** It is reported separately, counted separately, and never
averaged into a score. A subject with six gaps and two scores has a *coverage* problem that the
artifact must state on its face.

**Thin-record rule (non-negotiable).** Where a subject has no public record on a criterion,
mark `—`. Do not manufacture a plausible score with a hedging footnote. Fabricated-but-hedged
scores are the single most attackable element any scorecard can contain, and honest gaps are
more defensible than complete-looking tables.

## 4. Axes

An **axis** is one benchmark question applied consistently across subjects. Axes are the unit
of work, the unit of scoring, and the unit measured by the viability gate.

Candidate axis set (v0.1 — one is selected for the gate, the rest wait on its result):

| ID | Axis | Frame | Question put to every subject |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Enumerated authority | F1 | Did the action stay within the powers this body was actually granted? |
| **A2** | Proper object of government | F1/F5 | Is this an object of government at all, at *this* level? |
| **A3** | Subsidiarity | F1/F3 | Was it decided at the lowest competent level, or pulled upward? |
| **A4** | Property and contract | F2/F5 | Does it secure property and contract, or condition them on discretion? |
| **A5** | Public purse discipline | F5 | Does it spend general funds on a particular benefit? |
| **A6** | Process and consent | F1/F3 | Was it adopted with real deliberation and a recorded vote, or buried in consent? |

**A2 is the recommended axis for the viability gate.** It is the axis Pierre Review's
instrument structurally cannot ask, it is the one the DOGE reviews already exercise, and it is
the hardest to score — which is the right thing to time. If it is fast, everything is fast.

## 5. Scoring procedure

0. **Verify the roster first.** Never score a name pulled from memory or a prior draft. Confirm
   the current officeholder from an authoritative primary source (Secretary of State, official
   minutes, LRC). For SD local work, filter on "South Dakota" or "Codington County" — the
   Watertown, Wisconsin collision has produced roster errors before, and a roster error
   invalidates the entire document.
1. **Pin the axis and the frame** before looking at any subject.
2. **Collect the citable action** — a recorded vote, a written position, a quoted statement.
   No score without one.
3. **Cite the benchmark passage** — specific, retrievable, free.
4. **Place the band**, and write the one-sentence reason a reader could disagree with.
5. **Mark gaps as gaps.**
6. **Hand-check the arithmetic.** Re-add every column; recompute every percentage from its base
   numbers. A published weighting table whose weights do not sum correctly discredits the
   entire instrument regardless of how good the analysis was.

---

## 6. Required artifact structure

1. Scope, prepared date, and the **authorship disclosure** — first page, above the scores
2. The benchmark frames used, with end-dates and rationale
3. The scale, in full
4. The axis definitions actually applied
5. Scored table: subject × axis, with a distinct, visible gap column
6. Evidence citations, one per score
7. **"What This Scorecard Could Not Determine"** — every gap, every limit of the record, and the
   correction of any circulating claim on the operator's own side that started from wrong facts
8. Sources — every document named, every figure traceable

Section 7 is the credibility engine. Written thin, it takes the rest of the document down with it.

---

## 7. Firewall: this is not the fiscal instrument

The **Watertown DOGE fiscal review** scores no individual official, by design. Its power comes
from being an itemized, sourced, re-runnable set of questions that a hostile reader cannot
dismiss as a weighting exercise.

**Never import benchmark scores into a fiscal review, and never import fiscal-review findings
into a scorecard as if they were scores.** They may cite each other. They do not merge. A
fiscal review carrying scores is a campaign document wearing an auditor's coat, and it forfeits
the exact property that made it survivable.
