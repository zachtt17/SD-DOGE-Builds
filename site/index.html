South Dakota · school board to statehouse

A normative instrument for South Dakota government

Not what happened. Whether it was a proper object of the government that did it.

Every bill, vote, and budget line in this state is already tracked somewhere. What nobody publishes is a scored answer to the older question: is this a proper object of this government, at this level, measured against a declared standard with a declared end-date? That is what this site does, from the Watertown school board to the Legislature, with every number traceable to a public record you can pull yourself.

Three commitments. The rubric's authorship is disclosed on page one of every artifact. A subject with no public record on a criterion is marked as a gap, never given a made-up score. Every artifact carries a section titled What This Could Not Verify, and it is written thick.

Two instruments, kept apart

A fiscal review and a scorecard are different tools

A fiscal review is an itemized, sourced set of questions about spending. It scores no individual official, by design, because that is what makes it impossible for a hostile reader to dismiss as a weighting exercise.

A scorecard scores officials and measures against a declared and openly ideological benchmark, with the rubric and every input published so a critic can re-run it and disagree with the weighting rather than the facts.

A fiscal review that imports scores becomes a campaign document. A scorecard that hides its authorship becomes an attackable one. Nothing on this site does either.

Authorship. Prepared by Zach Thompson, Watertown, South Dakota. The benchmark is classical-liberal and Protestant in derivation and is disclosed as such on every artifact. This is not neutral civic education and does not claim to be.

Fiscal reviews score no individual official. Scorecards score individuals against a published, re-runnable rubric. The two are never merged.

Questions presume no wrongdoing. Categories flag constitutional weight; they do not declare illegality.
