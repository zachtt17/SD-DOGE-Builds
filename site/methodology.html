How the instrument works

Everything you need to re-run any number on this site and disagree with it on the merits.
1 · Who wrote this, and from where

Authorship, disclosed

The benchmark used here is classical-liberal and Protestant in derivation. It draws on the ratification debates, the early Republic's own construction of its Constitution, the common-law tradition through Story, the common-school settlement, and the economics of Smith, Bastiat, and the Austrians. That is a standpoint, not a neutral vantage, and it is stated on the first page of every artifact.

We disclose it because the alternative is worse. A scorecard that presents an ideological rubric as neutral civic education is more damaging when its lineage is traced than an openly held one ever is. There is no neutral taxonomy. Every scoring instrument encodes a frame. The honest choices are to declare yours or to leave it undeclared.

2 · What this instrument claims

Correspondence, not virtue

This instrument claims that a specific public action can be compared to a specific, cited, dated argument from the Western constitutional tradition, and that the degree of correspondence can be stated on a published scale.

It does not claim that the benchmark is neutral, that the founders agreed among themselves, that conformance equals legality, or that a low score means bad faith. It measures correspondence to a declared standard. It does not measure character.

3 · The benchmark frames

Five frames, each with a closing date

A frame without a stated end-date is an invitation to cherry-pick across two centuries. Each frame below says where its record closes and why.

FrameRecord closesWhy there
F1 Legal-structural1790sThe ratification debate and the First Congress legislating under the text it had just ratified, through the Bill of Rights and the Alien and Sedition controversy. After this the ratifiers are no longer the actors.
F2 JurisprudenceStory (1833; d. 1845)The last systematic constitutional commentary written by a jurist in living professional contact with the founding generation. After Story, interpretation is downstream of the sectional crisis.
F3 Civic1860sThe union, citizenship, and equal-protection questions the founding left unresolved are settled here. A civic benchmark that stops in 1789 cannot speak to citizenship, and would be dishonest by omission.
F4 Education1880sCovers the common-school settlement and its contemporaneous critics, and closes before Progressive-era professionalization, which is the development the frame exists to evaluate and so cannot be part of the standard.
F5 EconomicsOpen, through the AustriansThe one frame that is a continuous research program rather than a closed record: Smith, Bastiat, Menger, Mises, Hayek. The openness is declared, and the earliest adequate statement of any principle is the one cited.

Corpus rule. Founding-era citations come from public corpora: Founders Online, the Avalon Project, the Library of Congress, and the Anti-Federalist papers. If a critic cannot reach a cited passage for free, the citation does not ship.

4 · The scale

Five bands and a gap marker

+2Conforms. The action tracks a cited benchmark argument in principle and effect.+1Leans conforming. Consistent with the benchmark; effect or reasoning partly untested.0Indeterminate. The benchmark record is genuinely divided, or the action is orthogonal to it.−1Leans departing. In tension with a cited benchmark argument.−2Departs. Contradicts a cited benchmark argument in principle and effect.
—Insufficient public record. No citable public action on this criterion.
The gap marker is not a zero. It is reported separately, counted separately, and never averaged into a score. Where a subject has no public record on a criterion, we mark the gap. We do not manufacture a plausible score with a hedging footnote. Fabricated-but-hedged scores are the single most attackable element any scorecard can contain, and an honest gap is more defensible than a complete-looking table.
5 · The questions put to every subject

Axes

An axis is one benchmark question applied consistently across subjects. It is the unit of scoring.

AxisFrameThe question

A2 Proper objectF1 / F5Is this an object of government at all, at this level?
A3 SubsidiarityF1 / F3Was it decided at the lowest competent level, or pulled upward?

6 · For budgets

The four lenses

A year-over-year delta is a subtraction, not an analysis. Every budget line on this site is read four ways before anything is said about it, and no line is characterized as a trend on fewer than five years.

LensWhat it asksHow
L1 NominalWhat changed on paper?Straight from the adopted document, cited by page.
L2 RealIs the change real or an artifact of the dollar?Both years in constant dollars; one named index with its vintage for the whole document.
L3 Per capitaIs the change real or an artifact of population?Divided by the served population where it differs from the state's, with the source and vintage named.
L4 Proper objectIs this an object of this government at all?Three categories, applied to lines, never guessed for a category that hides many lines.

Clearly governmental   Gray zone   Constitutionally fraught   Requires line review

The categories flag constitutional weight. They do not declare illegality, and every artifact says so in those words. A "fraught" line is one where public funds go to a particular private benefit, in the tradition of Davy Crockett's Not Yours to Give; it is not an accusation that anyone broke a law.

Two more rules from the fiscal-review method. "Modern tools could do this cheaper" is a legitimate critique of data work and never of physical work, where the cost is dirt and labor. And where a descriptive label and an accounting code disagree, we state a documented question, never an accusation.

7 · What every artifact contains

The required structure

  1. Scope, prepared date, and the authorship disclosure, first page, above any score
  2. The frames used, with end-dates
  3. The scale, in full
  4. The axes actually applied
  5. The scored table, with a distinct and visible gap column
  6. One citation per score, retrievable for free
  7. What This Could Not Verify: every gap, every limit of the record, and the correction of any circulating claim on our own side that started from wrong facts
  8. Sources, every document named, every figure traceable

Item seven is the credibility engine. Written thin, it takes the rest down with it.

8 · If you think a score is wrong

Re-run it

The rubric, the weights, the per-item table, and the sources publish with every artifact. Pull the same records, apply the same scale, and show where the placement differs. If the disagreement is about the weighting, that is an argument we are glad to have in public. If it is about the facts, send the record and we correct the artifact and say so on its face.

Mechanical errors count. Every table is re-added by hand before release and every percentage recomputed from its base numbers; a published table whose figures do not reconcile discredits the instrument regardless of the analysis behind it. If you find one, it is fixed and noted.

9 · Roster

Nobody is scored from memory

Before any subject is scored, the current officeholder is confirmed from an authoritative primary source: the Secretary of State, official minutes, or the Legislative Research Council. A roster error invalidates the whole document, so this step is never skipped.
